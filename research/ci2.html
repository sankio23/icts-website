Research Unit

CI²

Complexity and Information Initiative

Based at the Sophia University Institute

Information and complexity have become foundational concepts across contemporary science. From quantum physics and computer science to biology and the social sciences, they increasingly function not only as analytical tools but as organising principles through which reality is described and explained.

Yet their scientific success has not been matched by conceptual clarity. Although widely employed across disciplines, information and complexity often carry different meanings, theoretical roles and ontological assumptions depending on the context in which they are used. As a result, concepts that appear to unify scientific knowledge frequently remain fragmented at the epistemological level.

CI² addresses this challenge by investigating the epistemic and ontological status of information and complexity as cross-cutting concepts. Bringing together philosophy of science, physics, computer science and complexity studies, the Unit develops a transdisciplinary framework capable of connecting different domains of knowledge through shared conceptual foundations.

Central question

How can information and complexity function as bridging concepts that structure scientific knowledge across disciplines, while offering new forms of explanation for increasingly interconnected systems?

Core research axes

  • 01

    Information in physical theories

    The role of information in quantum mechanics, statistical physics and theories of gravity, with attention to its foundational and explanatory significance.

  • 02

    Complexity and computation

    Algorithmic complexity, computability, prediction, and the limits of computational models in describing complex natural and artificial systems.

  • 03

    Epistemology of information

    Information as both an epistemic and an ontological concept, and its status across scientific theories and philosophical traditions.

  • 04

    Cross-domain applications

    Informational and complexity-based models applicable across disciplines, with attention to social, cognitive and socio-technical systems.

Signature initiatives

  • Foundations seminar — a permanent international seminar on the epistemic and ontological status of information across physical, computational and social theories.
  • Cross-domain modelling programme — collaborative development of informational and complexity-based models transferable between natural, cognitive and socio-technical systems.
  • International doctoral network — joint supervision, mobility and advanced training with partner institutions in Europe, Asia and Latin America.
