Research Unit

CeC

Christianity and Culture

Modernity, democracy, political theology and philosophies of peace · Sophia University Institute

Modernity has often been interpreted as the progressive emancipation of society from Christianity. Yet many of the concepts that shape contemporary democratic life — human dignity, freedom, conscience, the common good — remain deeply connected to the Christian intellectual tradition. Understanding this relationship is essential for interpreting both the achievements and the crises of contemporary societies.

The Christianity and Culture Unit investigates the role of Christianity as a culturally generative force in the modern and post-secular world. Rather than approaching Christianity as either a residual heritage or an ideological identity, the Unit explores how the Christian tradition has contributed to the formation of modern political, cultural and social institutions, while continuing to offer intellectual resources for addressing contemporary challenges.

Drawing on history, philosophy, political theology, religious studies and peace studies, CeC develops a transdisciplinary approach to the genealogy of modernity, the foundations of democracy, the limits of political power and the cultural conditions for peace and coexistence.

Central question

How can Christianity remain a source of cultural creativity, democratic renewal and peaceful coexistence within increasingly pluralistic and post-secular societies?

Core research axes

  • 01

    Christianity and modernity

    A genealogy of the modern age and its self-understanding: legitimacy and critique, secularisation and nihilism, Guardini's diagnosis of the end of the modern age, and the relationship between faith and autonomous reason.

  • 02

    Personalism

    The person as a central category of twentieth-century Christian thought; the distinction between individual and person and the relational ontology that sustains it; personalism as a third way between liberal individualism and collectivism.

  • 03

    Christianity and democracy

    The contested relationship between Christian anthropology and the democratic order; dignity and the common good; the place of religious conviction in a pluralist public sphere.

  • 04

    Theology and politics

    The theological-political problem and its principal positions, from Schmitt and Peterson to Maritain and contemporary political theology; eschatology and the proper limits of politics.

  • 05

    Christianity and philosophies of peace

    Peace as more than the absence of conflict; the contribution of Christian thought and of the tradition of unity proper to Sophia; fraternity, reconciliation, and holding differences together without violence.

Signature initiatives

  • Permanent seminar on Christianity, Modernity and Politics — an ongoing international seminar on the relationships between Christian thought, democratic institutions and the cultural transformations of modernity.
  • Critical editions and translations — scholarly editions and translations of works central to the genealogy of modern political and religious thought.
  • Public dialogues on religion and democracy — lectures and cultural events contributing to informed debate on the public role of Christianity in pluralistic societies.
