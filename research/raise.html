Research Unit

RAISE

Responsible AI and Social Ethics

Based at the Sophia University Institute

Artificial intelligence is transforming the conditions under which human beings act, decide and relate. Current approaches to AI governance are structured around two main axes: regulation and ethical frameworks. Regulation defines constraints and responsibilities at the institutional level; ethical principles articulate normative orientations for the design and use of technological systems. Both are necessary. Yet both remain insufficient.

They do not fully reach the level at which human action actually takes place: the level of judgment, responsibility and lived decision-making. In AI-mediated environments human agency is not removed but reconfigured. Decisions remain formally human, while the processes through which they are formed are increasingly filtered, suggested or partially automated.

This transformation generates a structural gap between governance and action. Norms exist, but the conditions for assuming responsibility are weakened. RAISE addresses this gap by treating responsibility not as compliance, but as a lived and public commitment.

Central question

How can human agents remain present to their actions in contexts where decision-making is mediated, accelerated and partially automated?

Core research axes

  • 01

    AI governance beyond compliance

    Analysis of the limits of regulation and ethical frameworks, and development of a third dimension of governance grounded in human responsibility.

  • 02

    Responsibility, agency and judgment

    Investigation of how AI mediates decision-making and transforms the conditions of human agency.

  • 03

    Public commitment and an oath

    Study of promise, commitment and the performative structures of responsibility, with particular attention to the role of the oath.

  • 04

    Formation and transformation

    Development of educational pathways, professional training and communities of practice sustaining responsible agency.

  • 05

    Applied domains

    Implementation and testing in education, organisations and companies, public institutions, and interreligious and peace contexts.

Signature initiatives

  • Oath-based commitment frameworks — the design of performative instruments through which professionals and institutions publicly assume responsibility for AI-mediated decisions.
  • Applied ethical toolkits — decision-making protocols and guidelines for organisations, public institutions and educational settings.
  • Executive and professional training — programmes connecting research on responsible agency with the practice of those who deploy AI systems.
