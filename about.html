The Thesis

Disciplines remain indispensable. They are no longer sufficient.

The International Center for Transdisciplinary Studies is an inter-university research center dedicated to advancing transdisciplinary approaches to the most complex challenges of our time. It was founded in 2026 by the Sophia University Institute (Italy) and the Tabosa de Almeida ASCES-UNITA University Center (Brazil).

From the Director

A research center is first of all a decision about where to look. ICTS has been founded on the conviction that the questions which now matter most are situated precisely where our institutions are least equipped to see them: between disciplines, between levels of description, between traditions of knowledge.

Behind this decision lies a diagnosis. The contemporary university has inherited two kinds of sharply drawn boundaries: those that separate the disciplines from one another, and those that separate the university itself from the other agents of knowledge — enterprises, public institutions, communities, citizens. Both boundaries have been historically productive; both have become, for a certain class of questions, an obstacle. ICTS was founded to give institutional form to their crossing: not to dissolve the university, but to equip it with structures in which the disciplines, and the university itself, can enter into productive relation with what lies beyond them.

Marco Sanchioni · Director, ICTS

The Center

As an inter-university center, ICTS does not replace the institutions that compose it. It provides the shared scientific structure through which their researchers work together across disciplinary and institutional boundaries, bringing into relation scholars, institutions and traditions of knowledge from different cultural contexts.

Rather than treating disciplinary boundaries as fixed divisions, ICTS approaches them as thresholds through which questions, methods and perspectives can move and interact. In doing so, the Center seeks to cultivate a research culture capable of engaging complexity without fragmentation, and pursuing unity without uniformity.

The Center is inter-university by construction rather than by aspiration. Its administrative headquarters are located at the Sophia University Institute, while its Units and Research Groups are hosted by partner institutions across five continents. Governance is exercised collegially through four distinct bodies.

The Relational Turn

Three ways of crossing a boundary. Each has its place in the Center's work — and the third defines it.

Multidisciplinarity · juxtaposition. Disciplines address a common theme, each from its own vantage point, and the results are placed side by side — as when economists, physicians and sociologists speak in turn on ageing. Knowledge is aggregated rather than transformed: each field retains its language, its methods and its criteria of validation. The boundary is respected; nothing crosses it.

Interdisciplinarity · integration. Concepts, methods and data migrate from one field to another — game theory into biology, network analysis into history, information theory into physics. The boundary is crossed, and the crossing is often fruitful; but the disciplines that lend and borrow return to their own questions essentially unchanged.

Transdisciplinarity · generation. The object of inquiry is itself relational: it exists in none of the contributing fields, and constituting it modifies each of them. Intelligence, care, cohesion and peace are objects of this kind. Here transdisciplinarity is no longer a method but an epistemic space — the level at which knowledge becomes generative, and at which ICTS situates its work.

Three Commitments

The word itself has a history. It entered the academic debate in 1970, at the OECD conference on interdisciplinarity in universities, where Jean Piaget described transdisciplinarity as a stage of knowledge no longer bounded by stable disciplinary borders. It reached its mature formulation in the 1990s with Basarab Nicolescu: transdisciplinarity not as a method but as an epistemic space — one in which inquiry moves across distinct levels of reality, involves the knowing subject, and becomes generative, producing meaning and not only information.

Transdisciplinary research, as ICTS understands it, is the disciplined construction of such objects. It requires three commitments. First, disciplinary competence: no relational object can be constructed by scholars who have not mastered at least one rigorous tradition. Second, conceptual translation: the shared work of establishing what a term means when it travels — "information", "responsibility", "person", "peace". Third, institutional form: environments in which such work is sustained over years rather than improvised in a workshop, and in which different subjects — universities, public institutions, enterprises, communities — can take part in inquiry.

M. Sanchioni, "Il ruolo della transdisciplinarità nell'era dell'intelligenza artificiale (generativa)", Sophia XVII (2025-1), pp. 69–94.

In pursuing this mission, the Center

— develops transdisciplinary research programmes on major global challenges;
— hosts international research units and laboratories;
— promotes collaboration across academic institutions;
— supports doctoral and postdoctoral research;
— engages with institutional, cultural and societal partners.

What ICTS produces

Six forms of output.

Common to every Research Unit and Group. Each structure additionally pursues its own signature initiatives.

  • 01

    Scholarly publication

    Peer-reviewed articles, book chapters, edited volumes, monographs and critical editions, published with international presses and journals across the Center's fields of inquiry.

  • 02

    International convening

    Workshops, conferences and permanent research seminars bringing together scholars from different disciplines, institutions and academic traditions.

  • 03

    Applied frameworks and policy

    Evidence-based reports, policy briefs and practical instruments — ethical guidelines, decision protocols, commitment frameworks — for public institutions, international organisations and professional practice.

  • 04

    Research networks

    Long-term partnerships and communities of practice connecting universities, research centers, public institutions and civil society organisations across continents.

  • 05

    Doctoral and postdoctoral formation

    Interdisciplinary supervision, advanced seminars, research mobility, executive education and international training opportunities integrated with the Units.

  • 06

    Public engagement

    Lectures, public dialogues, cultural events and third-mission initiatives contributing to informed debate on the questions the Center studies.
