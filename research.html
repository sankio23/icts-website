Research Architecture

Units, Groups and Strategic Initiatives.

Research at ICTS is organised through a small number of stable structures — designed so that disciplinary depth and transdisciplinary exploration sustain one another. Three concentric levels of institutional consolidation, echoing the three arcs of the Center's mark: constituted Units, active Groups, and emerging Strategic Initiatives.

Research Units

Three permanent structures.

A Research Unit is a permanent structure with its own host institution, research programme and partners. Each is built around a single relational object, and each is based at a host institution of the Center.

  • RAISE

    Responsible AI and Social Ethics

    RAISE treats responsibility not as compliance but as a lived and public commitment, addressing the gap that opens between AI governance and the level at which human action actually takes place.

  • CI²

    Complexity and Information Initiative

    CI² investigates the epistemic and ontological status of information and complexity as cross-cutting concepts, building a transdisciplinary framework across physics, computation and the social sciences.

  • CeC

    Christianity and Culture

    CeC investigates the role of Christianity as a culturally generative force in the modern and post-secular world — neither as residual heritage nor as ideological identity.

Research Groups

Four constituted teams.

A Research Group is a constituted team pursuing a defined research programme, normally within a broader thematic area, across institutions and continents. Research Groups may, over time, be constituted as Research Units by decision of the Council Board.

  • FRATERNITAS

    Fraternity, Politics and Social Theory

    FRATERNITAS treats relational categories — fraternity, solidarity, recognition — not as moral aspirations but as analytical tools for the study of democratic life.

  • CHARIS

    Charismatic Processes, Institutions and Social Transformation

    CHARIS examines charisma not as a theological or personal quality but as a historical and social process through which a founding experience generates enduring communities and institutions.

Strategic Research Initiatives

Three emerging domains.

A Strategic Initiative is an emerging domain in which new groups, projects and institutional networks may gradually take shape. These are spaces of exploration extending the Center's relational approach; new Research Groups are constituted from them by decision of the Council Board.

  • Human Development

    Human Development and New Relational Paradigms

    Contemporary challenges such as inequality, environmental degradation and social fragmentation have made visible the limits of development models built on aggregate economic indicators. This domain explores development as a relational process — shaped by public policies, institutional arrangements and community participation, and evaluated by the quality of the bonds it creates as much as by the resources it distributes.

  • OIKOS

    Family, Generations and Communion

    Family relationships, intergenerational dynamics and forms of communal life are undergoing significant transformations. This domain explores the family not only as a social institution but as a primary space of relational formation, reciprocity and solidarity — the setting in which the capacity for relation is first acquired and from which it is carried into civic life. It investigates communion as a social principle sustaining cohesion and civic participation.

  • Civil Economy

    Civil Economy and the Economy of Communion

    Economic systems are increasingly challenged to reconcile productivity, social justice, environmental sustainability and human dignity. The difficulty is not only technical: it concerns the conception of the economic agent on which standard models rest. Drawing on the tradition of civil economy and on the experience of the Economy of Communion, this domain investigates responsible entrepreneurship, cooperative enterprise and the economics of the common good.

Knowledge emerges in relations — between persons, between levels of reality, between visions of the world.
